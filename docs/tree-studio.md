# Small Groves

A forest begins as a gesture. Each tap releases a small family of trees, related by a palette and lighting direction but separated by seeded variations in crown proportions, silhouette, tilt, trunk width, and facet colour. The seed makes each gesture reproducible; randomness is a material rather than a substitute for composition.

The trees are built from coarse three-dimensional meshes projected onto white paper. A meticulously crafted algorithm gives each triangular face a colour derived from its orientation to a shared light. The limited geometry makes shape and light legible even at small sizes, while careful variation avoids the repetition of a stamped icon.

A grove distributes ten to twenty trees through an elliptical field around the gesture. Ground positions determine painting order, so trees overlap like a small landscape rather than an unordered pile. This is painstaking optimization in service of a simple interaction: a phone tap should feel immediate, with no continuous animation consuming its battery.

The composition accumulates rather than regenerates without consent. Undo removes one gesture, while a new seed explores another interpretation of the same planting pattern. The implementation preserves the artist's parameters and gesture history locally, and exports an image without making that composition public. The craft lies in coherent light, a restrained palette, and a responsive instrument that disappears behind the act of planting.

The viewer adapts the algorithmic-art skill's seed/parameter/action structure to EXBT's established white canvas and Courier typography. Its native Canvas renderer preserves the site's first-party-only, lightweight loading requirements.
